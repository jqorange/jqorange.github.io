About Me

Jiaqi Shen 沈佳琦

I am a Ph.D. student in the Department of Biomedical Engineering at Cornell University, working with the Fernández-Ruiz and Oliva Labs (AYALAB).

My research lies at the intersection of systems neuroscience, computational neuroscience, and machine learning. I am particularly interested in how the hippocampus encodes spatial and behavioral information during naturalistic behavior, and how the structure and dimensionality of neural population activity change across environments.

My work combines large-scale neural recordings, multimodal behavioral measurements, statistical modeling, and computational simulations. More broadly, I am interested in neural dynamics, plasticity, learning, and the computational principles underlying adaptive neural representations.

Before beginning my Ph.D., I received an M.Eng. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Cornell University and a B.S. in Artificial Intelligence from Huazhong University of Science and Technology.

Research

My current research focuses on hippocampal representations during naturalistic behavior, with an emphasis on connecting population-level neural activity to behavioral and environmental variables.

Hippocampus Population Coding

Neural Representations in Naturalistic Environments

Investigating how hippocampal CA1 neurons encode spatial and behavioral variables during indoor and outdoor foraging. I study changes in place-cell representations, mixed selectivity, and the dimensionality of neural population activity across different environments.

Behavior Sensor Fusion

Multimodal Behavioral Quantification

Developing computational pipelines that integrate inertial measurements, pose estimation, spatial tracking, and head-mounted video to characterize rodent behavior. This includes behavioral classification and relating movement, posture, and behavioral states to neural activity.

Computational Neuroscience Neural Coding

Computational Models of Neural Representations

Using encoding models and computational simulations to understand how spatial structure and behavioral modulation shape hippocampal population activity. My work examines place-field organization, mixed selectivity, population dimensionality, and the mechanisms that may generate these representations.

Research Interests Neural Dynamics

Learning, Plasticity, and Network Dynamics

I am also interested in how neural networks learn and reorganize their internal dynamics. Current exploratory interests include recurrent neural networks, dynamical criticality, representation learning, and the relationship between plasticity and adaptive computation.
